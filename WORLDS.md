# World map: when we teleport, and why it makes sense

The film is a **tour**. Clawd is the tourist; the narrator ("I", the essay's author) is the tour guide we only hear.
Viewers should always know three things: **where we are**, **why we came here**, and **what in this world stands for
what in the network**. These rules make every switch feel earned instead of random.

## The five switching rules

1. **Switch only when the story switches.** A new world starts only at a chapter boundary, on the line where the
   narration changes subject (the "bridge line" below). Never mid-thought. Inside a chapter, scene changes happen
   *within* the world, using that world's own device (see "in-world cuts").
2. **Every world is chosen for a reason the viewer can feel.** Mystery → noir. Growth → botany. Testing → a scoreboard.
   The world *is* the metaphor for that chapter, so the style itself explains something.
3. **Something always carries through the portal.** One object from the old world travels into the new one (a photo,
   the Knot, the lantern-lens), so the eye has a thread to follow. Listed per switch below.
4. **The same five things look the same everywhere.** Each world redraws them in its own style, but their shape, colour
   and role never change:
   - **a neuron** = something with *windows that show its photos*, glowing when it fires (a house, a book, a leaf, a podium)
   - **the Knot** = superposition: the same trefoil rope creature with eyes; it shrinks a little each time it is proved wrong
   - **gold** = generalization / clean; **violet** = superposition / knot (in every world's palette)
   - **the lens** = our tools (microscope, camera, telescope): fogged when tools are bad, clear when good
   - **Clawd** = the viewer's stand-in: reacts, never explains
5. **Arrive, then explain.** Each world opens with a 2–3 s establishing shot and a **passport stamp** in Clawd's
   passport (chapter number, world name). At the three act breaks, the passport opens to the **itinerary map**: a
   painted map of all the stops, a dotted line showing where we've been. That's the "you are here" for the whole film.

## The portal itself
- Until chapter 3 it is a **ragged tear** in the world (we don't know its shape yet).
- In chapter 3 the noir spotlight reveals that every case photo is a V, and the tear on the wall snaps into a **V**.
- From then on, every portal is V-shaped. The film's central idea becomes its transition device.

## Length and pacing
- Every world gets at least ~50 s (the shortest chapters are about that), so no world is a flash.
- Long chapters (10, 9, 7) stay in one world and use in-world cuts, so the viewer is never teleported twice in a row.
- 16 worlds over ~27 min is about one switch every 1.7 min, each one signposted by a stamp and a bridge line.
